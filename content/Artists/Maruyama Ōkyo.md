# Maruyama Ōkyo (円山 応挙)

**Lived**: 1733-1795

## Biography
- Born in Ano, Tanba Province (now Kyoto Prefecture)
- Initially trained as a doll maker
- Studied Chinese and Japanese painting traditions
- Founded the Maruyama school of painting
- Based in Kyoto, worked for temples, aristocracy, and wealthy merchants
- Created new approach to observational painting in Japan

## Artistic Innovations
- **Synthesis of Traditions**:
  - Combined elements of Chinese, Japanese, and Western approaches
  - Integrated Western perspective selectively into Japanese compositions
  - Balanced naturalistic observation with decorative elements
  
- **Observational Approach**:
  - Direct sketching from nature (shasei)
  - Detailed preliminary studies for finished works
  - Emphasis on accurate proportions and structures
  
- **Technical Innovations**:
  - Subtle gradations and modeling
  - Delicate ink wash techniques
  - Systematic approach to depicting complex forms

## Western Influence and Adaptation
- Studied Western prints and illustrations available in Kyoto
- Used elements of linear perspective but adapted to Japanese pictorial needs
- Incorporated chiaroscuro (light and shadow) selectively
- Created more volumetric figures than traditional Japanese painting
- Maintained Japanese compositional principles while adding Western elements

## Major Works
- **"Cracked Ice"** (1768): Screen painting combining realism with decorative pattern
- **"Pine Trees in Snow"** (1773): Screen showing naturalistic observation
- **"Sketches from Life Album"** (写生帖): Collection of observational studies
- **"Ghost of Oyuki"** (応挙幽霊図): Famous ghost painting
- **"Painting of a Cat"**: Demonstrates his observational approach to animals

## Commercial and Cultural Context
- Created works for both religious and secular patrons
- Designed for architectural contexts (screens, sliding doors)
- Established painting atelier with commercial structure
- Trained numerous students who continued his approach
- Works widely collected by temples, wealthy merchants, and aristocrats

## Influence on Later Design
- Maruyama-Shijō school continued his approach through 19th century
- Influenced later [[Kyoto Painting Schools]]
- Observational approach influenced aspects of later Japanese design
- Selective integration of Western and Eastern techniques became model for later artists

## Comparison to Other Approaches
- More naturalistic than [[Kano School]] painting
- Less directly Western-influenced than [[Dutch-Style Painting (Ranga)]]
- More elegant and refined than [[Ukiyo-e]] approaches
- Created bridge between court painting traditions and newer observational styles

## Relevance to Research Question
Maruyama Ōkyo exemplifies the sophisticated way Japanese artists selectively integrated Western visual techniques while maintaining distinctly Japanese aesthetic priorities. Unlike [[Shiba Kōkan]] who more explicitly adopted Western techniques, Ōkyo created a subtle synthesis that took specific Western elements (perspective, volume, observation) and incorporated them into a fundamentally Japanese pictorial approach. This selective adaptation demonstrates how Japanese visual culture actively shaped its engagement with Western techniques rather than passively receiving them. While Ōkyo worked primarily in fine art rather than commercial contexts, his approach of selective integration parallels similar processes in Edo commercial design, where designers incorporated specific Western elements while maintaining Japanese visual frameworks. This pattern challenges Western-centric narratives about innovation flowing primarily from West to East.
