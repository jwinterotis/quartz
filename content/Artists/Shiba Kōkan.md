# Shiba Kōkan (司馬 江漢)

**Lived**: 1747-1818

## Biography
- Born in Edo as Andō Kichirō
- Initially trained in traditional Japanese painting under [[Kanō Koshin]]
- Later studied under [[Suzuki Harunobu]] and created ukiyo-e prints
- Created forgeries of Harunobu's work after his death
- Became interested in Western knowledge and techniques in 1770s
- Changed name several times throughout career (Harushige, Kōkan, etc.)
- Traveled extensively within Japan documenting landscapes

## Western Knowledge and Techniques
- Learned Dutch language to access Western books
- Studied Western astronomy, geography, and perspective
- First Japanese artist to create copper-plate etchings (1783)
- Experimented with oil painting techniques
- Created the first Western-style landscape paintings in Japan
- Constructed camera obscura based on Dutch descriptions

## Major Works
- **"View of Mimeguri"** (1783): First Japanese copper-plate etching
- **"Self-Portrait"** (1783): One of earliest Japanese Western-style portraits
- **"Dutch Merchant Attending a Party"** (1790s): Oil painting
- **"Map of All Countries of the World"** (1796): Western-style world map
- **"Illustrated Discourse on the Earth"** (1793): Science publication with Western-style illustrations

## Intellectual Contributions
- Published books explaining Western astronomy and geography
- Promoted heliocentric model of solar system
- Introduced Western cartographic techniques
- Wrote extensively about Western art techniques
- Advocated empirical observation over traditional knowledge

## Position Between Traditions
- Moved between traditional Japanese and Western-style art
- Created both ukiyo-e woodblock prints and Western-style etchings
- Maintained elements of Japanese aesthetic while adopting Western techniques
- Approached Western techniques primarily for their scientific accuracy

## Commercial and Cultural Context
- Limited commercial market for Western-style works
- Found patronage among intellectual elites and domain lords
- Published books that successfully combined text and Western-style illustrations
- Connected to [[Rangaku]] (Dutch Learning) intellectual circles

## Legacy
- Pioneer of Western art techniques in Japan
- Influenced later Japanese artists interested in Western approaches
- Helped introduce concepts of perspective and realistic representation
- Important bridge figure between Edo period and later Meiji westernization
- Works preserved in major Japanese museums

## Relevance to Research Question
Shiba Kōkan represents a fascinating case study in cross-cultural visual influence during the Edo period. His work demonstrates how Japanese artists selectively adopted Western techniques while maintaining their own aesthetic and intellectual frameworks. Unlike later Meiji period artists who sometimes uncritically embraced Western approaches, Kōkan strategically incorporated specific Western elements that served his purposes while continuing to work within Japanese contexts. His dual facility with traditional ukiyo-e and Western-style techniques shows how Japanese artists could move between different visual languages rather than simply replacing one with the other. This selective adaptation challenges Western-centric narratives about innovation flowing primarily from West to East and demonstrates the agency and sophistication of Japanese visual culture during the Edo period.
