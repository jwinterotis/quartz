The **Modern Style** is a style of architecture, art, and design that first emerged in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") in the mid-1880s. It was the first [[Comparative Context/West/Art Nouveau]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau "Art Nouveau") style worldwide, and it represents the evolution of the [[Arts and Crafts]] movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_and_Crafts_movement "Arts and Crafts movement") which was native to [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"). The Modern Style provided the base and intellectual background for the Art Nouveau movement and was adapted by other countries, giving birth to local variants such as [Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugendstil "Jugendstil") and the [Vienna Secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Secession "Vienna Secession"). It was cultivated and disseminated through the [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_\(department_store\) "Liberty (department store)") department store and _[The Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Studio_\(magazine\) "The Studio (magazine)")_ magazine.
