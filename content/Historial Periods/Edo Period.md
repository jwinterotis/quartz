The **Edo period** (江戸時代, _Edo jidai_), also known as the **Tokugawa period** (徳川時代, _Tokugawa jidai_), is the period between 1600 or 1603 and 1868[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period#cite_note-1) in the [history of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Japan "History of Japan"), when the country was under the rule of the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate "Tokugawa shogunate") and some 300 regional _[daimyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimyo "Daimyo")_, or feudal lords. Emerging from the chaos of the [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period), the Edo period was characterized by prolonged peace and stability, urbanization and economic growth, strict social order, [isolationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationism "Isolationism") foreign policies, and popular enjoyment of [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_art "Japanese art") and [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Japan "Culture of Japan").
