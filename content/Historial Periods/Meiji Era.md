The **Meiji era** (明治時代, _Meiji jidai_, [[meꜜː(d)ʑi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese "Help:IPA/Japanese") [](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/64/TomJ-Meiji.ogg/TomJ-Meiji.ogg.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:TomJ-Meiji.ogg "File:TomJ-Meiji.ogg")) was an [era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_era_name "Japanese era name") of [Japanese history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Japan "History of Japan") that extended from October 23, 1868, to July 30, 1912.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era#cite_note-1) The Meiji era was the first half of the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan "Empire of Japan"), when the Japanese people moved from being an isolated [feudal society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) at risk of colonization by [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world "Western world") powers to the new paradigm of a modern, industrialized [nation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state "Nation state") and emergent [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power "Great power"), influenced by Western scientific, technological, philosophical, political, legal, and aesthetic ideas. As a result of such wholesale adoption of radically different ideas, the changes to Japan were profound, and affected its social structure, internal politics, economy, military, and foreign relations. The period corresponded to the reign of [Emperor Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Meiji "Emperor Meiji"). It was preceded by the [Keiō era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei%C5%8D_era "Keiō era") and was succeeded by the [Taishō era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D_era "Taishō era"), upon the accession of [Emperor Taishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taish%C5%8D "Emperor Taishō").
