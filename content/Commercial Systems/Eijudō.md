# Eijudō (栄寿堂)

Eijudō was the publishing house of Nishimura Yohachi, one of the most significant publishers of the late Edo period.

## Business History
- **Established**: Early 19th century
- **Founder**: Nishimura Yohachi (西村与八)
- **Location**: Bakuro-chō district of Edo
- **Active Period**: Flourished 1800s-1840s
- **Significance**: Major publisher of landscape prints and series

## Key Artist Relationships
- **[[Katsushika Hokusai]]**: Primary publisher of his landmark series
  - "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji"
  - "One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji"
  - "A Tour of Waterfalls in Various Provinces"
- **[[Andō Hiroshige]]**: Published some of his early landscape series
- **[[Keisai Eisen]]**: Published his bijinga and landscape works

## Commercial Innovations
- **Series Development**: Pioneer of thematic landscape series format
- **Long-term Artist Relationships**: Sustained collaboration with Hokusai
- **Technical Quality**: Known for high-standard printing
- **Marketing Innovation**: Developed subscription model for series
- **Cross-promotion**: Connected different series through branding

## Visual Identity
- **Publisher Seal**: Distinctive square seal with "Eijudō" characters
- **Product Lines**: Consistent formatting within series
- **Series Branding**: Distinctive cartouche designs for different series
- **Quality Positioning**: Positioned prints as premium products
- **Visual Continuity**: Maintained consistent quality and style

## Business Context
- **Market Expansion**: Capitalized on growth in landscape print popularity
- **Tourism Connection**: Prints connected to growing domestic tourism
- **Cultural Significance**: Published works that would define Japanese visual identity
- **Commercial Scale**: Large operation with multiple craftspeople
- **Distribution Network**: Sold throughout Edo and beyond

## Relevance to Research Question
Eijudō exemplifies the sophisticated publisher systems that enabled Edo period commercial design to flourish. Through strategic artist relationships, series development, visual branding, and technical excellence, Eijudō operated with a level of commercial design sophistication comparable to modern publishing houses. The fact that Eijudō published works like Hokusai's "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji"—which would later revolutionize Western visual arts when "discovered" by European artists—demonstrates how commercial systems in Edo Japan produced visual design of unprecedented sophistication and impact. The business model and branding approach used by Eijudō parallels modern design agency practices, yet developed independently in Japan over a century before similar Western developments.
