# Art Nouveau (1890-1910)

Art Nouveau was a decorative art movement that emerged in Europe in the 1890s and remained influential until around 1910.

## Key Characteristics
- Organic, flowing curvilinear forms
- Nature-inspired motifs (plants, flowers, insects)
- Asymmetrical compositions
- Integration of text and image
- Emphasis on flat, decorative surfaces
- Stylized, elongated figures

## Major Figures
- Alphonse Mucha (1860-1939)
- Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec (1864-1901)
- Aubrey Beardsley (1872-1898)
- Gustav Klimt (1862-1918)
- Hector Guimard (1867-1942)

## Japanese Influence
- **Direct Borrowing**: Artists openly acknowledged influence from [[Japanese Printing Techniques]]
- **Compositional Techniques**: Adopted asymmetrical balance from ukiyo-e prints
- **Flat Color**: Embraced Japanese approach to flat, decorative color fields
- **Nature Motifs**: Directly inspired by Japanese natural representations
- **Linearity**: Emphasized flowing line work similar to Japanese brush techniques

## Specific Japanese Sources
- **[[Katsushika Hokusai]]'s Wave Imagery**: Informed Art Nouveau's flowing organic lines
- **[[Andō Hiroshige]]'s Nature Scenes**: Inspired compositional approaches
- **[[Nishiki-e]] Color Techniques**: Influenced Art Nouveau's approach to flat color
- **Japanese Pattern Books**: Used as direct reference sources

## Commercial Applications
- Poster design
- Advertising
- Book and magazine illustration
- Architectural ornamentation
- Decorative arts and furniture

## Historical Context
- Coincided with Japan's opening to the West
- Followed the Japonisme craze in European art
- Reaction against academic art traditions
- Parallel to [[Arts and Crafts Movement]] but more ornamental

## Relevance to Research Question
Art Nouveau represents perhaps the clearest example of Western design directly borrowing principles from Edo period commercial design practices. Art Nouveau designers openly acknowledged their Japanese influences, particularly from ukiyo-e prints. Many design principles that made Art Nouveau seem "revolutionary" in Western contexts—asymmetrical composition, flat color, integration of text and image—had been standard in Japanese commercial design for over a century. This movement provides strong evidence that Western "modern" design directly appropriated established Japanese commercial approaches rather than inventing them independently.
