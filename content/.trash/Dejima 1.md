**Dejima** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 出島, lit. 'exit island') or **Deshima**,[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima#cite_note-1) in the 17th century also called **Tsukishima** (築島, lit. 'built island'), was an [artificial island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_island "Artificial island") off [[Nagasaki]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki "Nagasaki"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), that served as a [trading post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_post "Trading post") for the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire "Portuguese Empire") (1570–1639) and subsequently the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire "Dutch Empire") (1641–1858).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima#cite_note-2) For 220 years, it was the central conduit for foreign trade and cultural exchange with Japan during the isolationist [[Edo period]]
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period "Edo period") (1600–1869), and the only Japanese territory open to Westerners.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima#cite_note-3)
