# Bokashi (暈し)

Bokashi refers to a sophisticated Japanese woodblock printing technique that creates color gradations, adding depth, atmosphere, and subtle transitions to prints.

## Technical Process
- **Definition**: Color gradation technique in woodblock printing
- **Application Methods**:
  - **Brush Bokashi**: Hand-applied wiping of pigment on block
  - **Burnishing Bokashi**: Rubbing back areas after printing
  - **Ita-bokashi**: Gradation applied directly to block
  - **Surikomi-bokashi**: Overlapping multiple printings
- **Tools**: Special brushes, cloth, and applicators
- **Skill Level**: Required significant expertise from master printers

## Historical Development
- **Early Development**: Basic forms used in early 18th century
- **Refinement**: Sophisticated application by mid-18th century
- **Master Practitioners**: [[Suzuki Harunobu]], [[Katsushika Hokusai]], [[Andō Hiroshige]]
- **Peak Usage**: 1780s-1850s in landscape and atmospheric prints

## Commercial Applications
- **Atmospheric Effects**: Creating sky, water, and weather representations
- **Emotional Impact**: Enhancing mood through color transition
- **Visual Depth**: Creating sense of space and depth without Western perspective
- **Product Differentiation**: Premium technique that distinguished higher-quality prints

## Visual Effects Achieved
- **Sky and Water**: Subtle transitions in natural elements
- **Time Passage**: Dawn/dusk effects
- **Weather Conditions**: Mist, rain, snow atmospherics
- **Emotional Qualities**: Melancholy, tranquility, drama

## Comparison to Western Techniques
- **[[Mezzotint Technique]]**: Western approach to tonal gradation but limited to black/white and not commercially viable
- **[[Lithography]]**: Later Western technique that could achieve gradation but not until 19th century
- **Aquatint**: Western printmaking technique for tonal areas but limited commercial application
- **Watercolor Wash**: Manual technique used for book illustration but not mass-produced

## Examples of Masterful Usage
- **[[Andō Hiroshige]]'s "Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge"**: Rain effects
- **[[Katsushika Hokusai]]'s "Great Wave off Kanagawa"**: Subtle sky gradation
- **Evening scenes by various artists**: Dawn/dusk color transitions
- **[[Mountain landscapes]]**: Atmospheric distance effects

## Relevance to Research Question
Bokashi represents a sophisticated technical innovation that produced visual effects Western printing could not achieve until much later. While Western print techniques like mezzotint could produce tonal gradations, they were limited to fine art contexts and could not be applied to mass commercial production as bokashi was. The technique demonstrates how Japanese printers developed complex visual solutions specifically for commercial contexts, achieving effects that Western commercial printing could not match until the development of chromolithography and later photomechanical processes. This technical sophistication challenges Western narratives that place print innovation primarily in European contexts and shows how Japanese commercial design achieved visual effects that influenced later Western design when "discovered" in the late 19th century.
