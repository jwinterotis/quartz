# Dutch-Style Painting (Ranga, 蘭画)

Ranga ("Dutch pictures") refers to a style of Japanese painting that incorporated Western techniques learned through Dutch trading contacts during the Edo period.

## Historical Development
- **Origins**: Mid-18th century during sakoku (closed country) period
- **Information Sources**: 
  - Dutch books and prints entering via [[Dejima]]
  - Limited direct instruction from Dutch residents
  - Scientific and medical illustrations
- **Period of Florescence**: 1770s-1850s
- **Decline**: Gradually supplanted by photography and newer Western art movements after Japan's opening

## Key Technical Elements
- **Linear Perspective**: Systematic representation of spatial recession
- **Chiaroscuro**: Modeling of forms through light and shadow
- **Anatomical Accuracy**: Based on Western scientific understanding
- **Oil Paint**: Some artists experimented with oil-based media
- **Copper-plate Etching**: Printing technique introduced from the West

## Major Practitioners
- **[[Shiba Kōkan]]** (1747-1818):
  - Pioneer of Western-style techniques in Japan
  - First Japanese copper-plate etching
  - Created both paintings and prints
  - Published theoretical writings on Western art
  
- **[[Hiraga Gennai]]** (1728-1780):
  - Polymath who studied Dutch sciences
  - Early advocate of Western representational techniques
  - Connected scientific observation with visual representation
  
- **[[Aōdō Denzen]]** (1748-1822):
  - Developed color printing using Western-inspired techniques
  - Created landscape views using perspective
  
- **[[Satake Shozan]]** (1748-1785):
  - Samurai and painting theorist
  - Wrote on Western techniques of representation

## Relationship to Japanese Traditions
- **Contrast with [[Kano School]]**: Traditional Japanese painting emphasized line and pattern
- **Parallel to [[Maruyama-Shijō School]]**: Both sought greater realism but through different means
- **Integration Attempts**: Some artists combined Western techniques with Japanese aesthetics
- **Technical vs. Aesthetic Adoption**: Often adopted technical aspects while maintaining Japanese aesthetic priorities

## Commercial and Cultural Context
- **Limited Market**: Initially restricted to intellectual and scientific circles
- **Scientific Illustration**: Strong connection to medical and scientific publication
- **Elite Patronage**: Popular among forward-thinking daimyo and scholars
- **[[Rangaku]]** Connection: Part of broader interest in Dutch/Western knowledge

## Comparison to Contemporary Western Art
- **Japanese Ranga**: Focused on technical aspects of representation
- **Contemporary European Art**: Moving toward Romanticism and expression
- **Information Lag**: Japanese artists worked from dated European sources
- **Selective Adaptation**: Japanese artists chose elements that interested them rather than adopting entire Western tradition

## Relevance to Research Question
Ranga represents selective Japanese adaptation of Western visual techniques while maintaining distinctly Japanese aesthetic priorities and commercial contexts. Unlike Western artistic movements that claimed to break completely with past traditions, Ranga artists strategically incorporated specific Western techniques that served their purposes while continuing to operate within Japanese cultural frameworks. This selective adaptation demonstrates the sophisticated discernment of Japanese artists even with limited access to Western sources. While Ranga was not primarily commercial in nature, it exemplifies the pattern of Japanese artists independently evaluating and selectively adopting foreign techniques rather than being passive recipients of Western influence—a pattern that applies to commercial design as well. This challenges Western-centric narratives about innovation flowing primarily from West to East.
